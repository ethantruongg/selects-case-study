Product decisions

The interesting part was deciding what not to build.

I kept the first version focused on the moments that mattered most in the photographer-client workflow, then made deliberate tradeoffs around usability, security, and operational control.

01 · BROWSING

Three ways to review

Grid, swipe, and lightbox modes share one live selection state, so clients can switch interaction models without losing their place.

02 · PRICING

Price while you select

Per-shoot pricing tiers update as the selection changes, making the cost of adding images visible before finalization.

03 · SECURITY

Access without accounts

Private links and PIN verification reduce login friction while rate limiting and temporary lockouts protect the public lookup path.

04 · DELIVERY

Release when ready

A manual release control separates photographer workflow from client visibility, giving the photographer the final say over delivery.

05 · MEDIA

Protect proofs upstream

Watermarked previews are generated during upload while original files remain private, trading retroactive flexibility for a simpler proofing pipeline.

06 · DEVICES

Download for the device

Desktop clients get ZIP delivery while mobile clients use the native share sheet, reflecting real platform constraints rather than fighting them.

Research → prioritization → iteration

Built with real workflows in mind.

I collected feedback from clients and photographers across both sides of the workflow, then used that feedback to prioritize improvements to photo selection and gallery management.

The goal was not to recreate every feature of a mature gallery platform. It was to understand where friction existed, make explicit product tradeoffs, and ship a focused v1.
